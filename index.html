What a will changes

Without a valid will, your estate is usually shared out by a fixed legal formula. A will lets you decide instead. Details vary by situation, so ask us about yours.

Who receives your assets

With a will

You choose each beneficiary and what they receive.

Without a will

The law's formula decides, which may not match your wishes.

Who cares for your children

With a will

You name the guardian you trust.

Without a will

A court may have to decide.

Who manages your estate

With a will

Your chosen executor acts on your instructions.

Without a will

Your family may need to apply to be appointed first.

What your family goes through

With a will

Clear instructions mean fewer delays and disagreements.

Without a will

More paperwork, more waiting, and more room for disputes.

Questions we hear most

Do I need a will if I don't own much?

Yes. A will also decides who looks after your children and who handles your affairs, whatever the size of your estate.

How long does it take?

It depends on how complex your situation is. Many straightforward wills can be drafted soon after we talk. Message us for an estimate.

Can I change my will later?

Yes. Update it whenever your circumstances change, for example after marriage, a new child or buying property.

Is my information confidential?

Yes. What you share is kept private and used only to prepare your documents.

How much does it cost?

It depends on what you need. Message us and we will quote clearly before you commit to anything.
